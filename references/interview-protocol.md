# Interview Protocol

## Purpose

The protocol collects concrete evidence for a dated profile. It is designed to
be conversational, not interrogative. Ask a small batch, reflect back what was
heard, and let the subject correct the interpretation.

## Before a Session

Establish:

- subject identity or pseudonym;
- whether this is self-profiling or authorized third-party profiling;
- the purpose and intended audience;
- permitted sources;
- sensitive topics that should be excluded;
- whether anything may be written to disk;
- the date and version of the latest profile, if one exists.

Do not begin with psychological labels or scores.

## Baseline Sequence

Use approximately six sessions or six focused blocks. A block may contain 5-10
questions from the question bank, depending on depth.

### Round 1: Timeline and Context

Map formative environments, transitions, constraints, and turning points.
Prioritize dates, places, people, decisions, and observable consequences.

### Round 2: Capability and Output

Identify what the person repeatedly does well, how they learn, and where effort
produces disproportionate results. Ask for artifacts or examples.

### Round 3: Cognition and Decisions

Reconstruct real decisions: what was noticed, what was ignored, what options
were considered, what trade-offs were made, and what happened afterward.

### Round 4: Motivation and Values

Look for enacted values rather than stated ideals. Ask what the person
protects, sacrifices, returns to, avoids, and refuses.

### Round 5: Social Roles and Environment

Compare behavior across collaboration, authority, conflict, feedback, and
different physical or institutional environments.

### Round 6: Contradictions and Future

Test competing explanations, add disconfirming evidence, and explore scenarios
that would change the current profile.

## Follow-Up Ladder

When an answer is abstract, move down this ladder:

1. Ask for a recent example.
2. Ask for a second example in a different context.
3. Ask what happened before and after.
4. Ask what the person chose not to do.
5. Ask what someone who disagrees might say.
6. Ask what evidence would prove the current interpretation wrong.

Useful follow-up stems:

- "What did you actually do next?"
- "What was the moment the decision became clear?"
- "What would have made you choose differently?"
- "Where does this pattern not hold?"
- "Who would describe the same event differently?"

## Evidence Capture

For each meaningful answer, capture:

- source type: self-report, artifact, third-party, observation, or public
  record;
- a short factual summary;
- an optional direct quote;
- the date and context;
- reliability: high, medium, or low;
- sensitivity: whether it should remain local or be excluded;
- the scale it informs: micro, meso, macro, temporal, or contextual.

Record interpretation separately from the evidence.

## Update Sessions

For an update:

1. Read the latest dated profile.
2. Ask what changed since that snapshot.
3. Collect new evidence before comparing it with earlier conclusions.
4. Mark confirmed, weakened, contradicted, and still-unknown conclusions.
5. Create a new snapshot; do not overwrite the previous one.
6. State the minimum next observation that would improve confidence.

## Stop Conditions

Pause or redirect the session when:

- the subject withdraws consent or becomes uncomfortable;
- the conversation moves into diagnosis, crisis care, or high-impact decisions;
- the subject asks for a total score or a deterministic identity label;
- a third party is discussed without a legitimate, authorized reason;
- sensitive details are not necessary for the stated purpose;
- the session is producing adjectives rather than evidence.

If there is an imminent safety concern, stop profiling and encourage the person
to contact appropriate local emergency or clinical support.

## Session Close

End with:

- a brief evidence-based reflection;
- at least one uncertainty;
- the subject's corrections;
- one question or small experiment for the next update.
